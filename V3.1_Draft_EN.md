## Abstract

This document is the V3.1 draft version of the TLAA architecture. While retaining all core designs of V3.0—tiered audit, three-layer memory, meta-audit closed loop, etc.—this draft further explores: ① extending the cognitive mode difference between the Thought Layer and the Execution Layer from "audit mode" to the level of "value orientation and role positioning," introducing guided review collaboration; ② establishing a dual-track information weighting system within the model for generation-oriented and logic-oriented evaluation, achieving decoupled assessment of review and generation; ③ exploring the use of mandatory context injection and fine-tunable audit preference vectors to shift logic verification from external supervision to internal constraints during the generation process; ④ establishing a four-tier progressive defense—Shared Expert Isolation, Dynamic Bias Adjustment, Auxiliary Loss, and Fixed Routing—against the structural risk of the Thought Layer being bypassed by routing in MoE architectures; ⑤ introducing Structural-Semantic Dual-Track Monitoring and dynamic degradation and rollback mechanisms to address the risk of the Thought Layer's own reasoning degradation; ⑥ filtering out non-logical content before it enters the Thought Layer through Semantic Scene Filtering, to avoid excessive review of such content.

**The core principle upheld by this draft: any Parameterized Cognitive Constraint must be premised on being "open to questioning," with "fidelity to facts" as the non-negotiable bottom line.** All draft content is derived from existing open-source components and cutting-edge research; additional costs lack quantitative data and require prototype validation.

### 4.1 Deepening of Cognitive Mode Difference: From "External Audit" to "Guided Review"

**Design Motivation**: V3.0 established the structural difference in cognitive modes between the Thought Layer and the Execution Layer. However, the "shallow audit" relying solely on low temperature and constrained steps is insufficient to fundamentally resolve the Execution Layer's "self-justification" trap. The relationship between the Thought Layer and the Execution Layer should not be merely an adversarial "find-fix" one, but should establish a constructive functional division—the Thought Layer assumes a guided review function, while the Execution Layer continuously improves output quality through corrections.

**Technical Implementation Path**:

- **Behavioral Cloning Initialization**: Use human-annotated collaborative dialogue data to perform behavioral cloning for both the Thought Layer and the Execution Layer, establishing a foundational functional division pattern.

- **GRPO Optimization**: Optimize audit feedback and correction behavior through Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) reinforcement learning. Existing research has shown that GRPO can induce process reward models under specific conditions, providing fine-grained optimization signals for audit behavior.

- **Role-Reversal Training**: Introduce fine-tuning data where the Thought Layer generates and the Execution Layer reviews, allowing each to understand the other's cognitive mode.

**Expected Value and Pending Validation**:

- Expected Value: Potential to reduce defensive generation by the Execution Layer and improve correction efficiency.

- Pending Validation: In highly abstract or innovative tasks, the role positioning framework may inhibit the Execution Layer from proposing breakthrough solutions, requiring diversity reward mechanisms to preserve independent exploration capability. The specific effects of role-reversal training require large-scale empirical validation.

### 4.2 Dual-Track Information Weighting System

**Design Motivation**: The Execution Layer's information weighting preferences during generation (token co-occurrence probability, semantic fluency) and the Thought Layer's information weighting preferences during review (causal chain completeness, evidence credibility) have fundamentally different objectives. If the two weighting systems are mixed, review conclusions may be disturbed by generation preferences.

**Dual-Track Weighting Design**:

| Dimension | Execution Layer Weighting (Generation-Oriented) | Thought Layer Weighting (Logic-Oriented) |
| - | - | - |
| **Core Objective** | Generate coherent, fluent text | Verify reasoning rigor, premise self-consistency |
| **Focus** | Token co-occurrence probability, semantic similarity | Causal chain completeness, evidence credibility, logical leaps |
| **Information Sources** | Pre-training parameters, context KV cache | User original intent, original memory, causal engine |
| **Working Mode** | Random sampling based on current distribution | Low temperature (\<0.3), constrained steps (≤2), conservative output |
| **Trigger Condition** | Always on | Activated at G2 and above |


When the two weighting systems produce significantly divergent scores for the same text segment, a "Weighting Divergence" state is flagged, triggering Meta-Audit or user adjudication in G4 scenarios.

**Pending Validation**: The dynamic coordination strategy for the two weighting systems may introduce additional computational overhead in large-scale deployments not yet verified. In certain scenarios (e.g., creative writing), logic-oriented weighting should actively yield to generation-oriented weighting; how to dynamically adjust priority is a question requiring further research in subsequent versions.

### 4.3 Parameterized Cognitive Constraints

**Design Motivation**: Post-hoc review alone cannot resolve the Execution Layer's inherent "fluency trap." It is necessary to establish internal constraints rooted in the model's generation process within the Execution Layer, enabling it to proactively avoid obvious logical vulnerabilities during generation. Multiple parallel technical paths exploring this direction already exist in academia, including embedding moral meaning processing directly into model architectures, and tracking and manipulating moral representations through Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs).

**Technical Implementation Paths**:

**Path One: Mandatory Context Injection**

- During each generation, inject core logic review rules (e.g., "ensure causal direction is correct, do not treat correlation as causation") as high-priority system prompts at the forefront of the Execution Layer's context.

- Advantages: Simple to implement, no additional training required.

- Limitations: Occupies context window; effectiveness diminishes if the model ignores long instructions during inference.

**Path Two: Fine-Tunable Audit Preference Vector**

- Through reinforcement learning methods such as GRPO, train rule-compliance behavior into the model's internal parameterized preferences.

- After training, the model automatically adjusts token probability distributions during generation, reducing the probability of logically suspect tokens being selected.

- Advantages: Internalized as model capability, does not occupy context.

- Limitations: Higher training cost; may generalize to simple tasks that do not require review.

**Anti-Extremism Constraint**: Neither mandatory context injection nor the audit preference vector should be set too extremely. Excessive cognitive constraints may cause the model to lose necessary creativity and flexibility. The intensity of constraints should be dynamically adjusted according to task type.

**⚠️ Inherent Risk Statement**: Any value orientation solidified as an internal model preference may evolve into a new source of bias. Existing research has termed this phenomenon "Core Belief Immunity"—even when a model possesses logical reasoning capabilities and recognizes its own blocking mechanisms, rational logical integration may still fail when conclusions threaten positions encoded as "legitimate" during training. Therefore, any Parameterized Cognitive Constraint must be premised on being "open to questioning." TLAA's Meta-Audit mechanism and user adjudication channel are precisely designed to prevent such self-enclosure of preferences—when the Thought Layer's review conclusions conflict with external evidence, the system should prioritize reporting to the user rather than self-adjudicating. It must be frankly acknowledged that no technical solution can completely eliminate this risk; it requires ongoing external oversight and human intervention.

**Pending Validation**: The generalization of audit preference vectors requires large-scale validation; how to dynamically reduce constraint intensity in highly abstract or innovative tasks remains to be determined. Drawing on the six-tier progressive architecture design approach of existing moral internalization frameworks, the training of Parameterized Cognitive Constraints should follow a gradual, tiered progression principle.

### 4.4 Defense Mechanisms Against Routing Bypass

**Problem Analysis**: Under high-load scenarios, the MoE gating network may bypass the Thought Layer expert module. Existing empirical research has revealed the severity of this risk—safety-critical behavior in MoE models is highly concentrated in a small number of experts, and selectively disabling only approximately 3% of neurons can significantly increase attack success rates.

**Four-Tier Progressive Defense Mechanism**:

| Defense Tier | Mechanism | Applicable Scenarios | Principle and Basis |
| - | - | - | - |
| **Tier 1: Shared Expert Isolation** | Deploy the Thought Layer as a continuously activated "Shared Expert" | G1/G2 routine review | DeepSeek MoE's Shared Experts are not affected by routing policies |
| **Tier 2: Dynamic Bias Adjustment** | Introduce a learnable dynamic bias term for Thought Layer experts; automatically increase when activation frequency is low | Training and fine-tuning, routine inference | Draws on DeepSeek V3's auxiliary-loss-free load balancing strategy |
| **Tier 3: Auxiliary Loss** | Add a load balancing loss term to the training objective | High-load / long-context scenarios | Explicitly penalizes the marginalization of Thought Layer experts |
| **Tier 4: Fixed Routing** | Directly set Thought Layer experts as mandatorily activated in the gating network | G4 complete review (high-risk) | Final fallback defense, trading possible inference overhead for guarantee |


**Implementation Priority**: Phase 1: Shared Expert Isolation (lowest cost); Phase 2: Dynamic Bias Adjustment; Phase 3: Auxiliary Loss; Phase 4: Fixed Routing (enabled only for high-risk scenarios).

**Pending Validation**: The annealing strategy for the dynamic bias update rate requires tuning for different scenarios; the selection of auxiliary loss coefficients lacks general guiding principles. In the long term, reliance on external routing constraints should be reduced through the enhancement of intrinsic model capabilities.

### 4.5 Thought Layer Reasoning Degradation Defense

**Problem Analysis**: Under high-load scenarios, the Thought Layer itself may experience reasoning degradation due to excessive cognitive load—manifested as perfunctory "surface review" or even "cognitive evasion." Humans produce cognitive "inertia" through the Hebbian process of updating beliefs via repeated exposure, whereas LLMs perform "massless reasoning," updating beliefs from scratch in principle with each inference. This difference both explains the deep mechanism of Thought Layer reasoning degradation under high load and provides a theoretical basis for designing monitoring metrics.

**Three-Tier Defense Mechanism**:

- **Tier 1: Structural-Semantic Dual-Track Monitoring**: Establish two parallel evaluation systems within the Thought Layer—traditional "logic scoring" and "structural monitoring" of the Thought Layer's own reasoning stability. When logic scores are high but structural monitoring metrics are abnormal (e.g., large numbers of repetitive patterns or reasoning leaps), flag as "suspected reasoning degradation."

- **Tier 2: Token Guard and Meta-Audit Cross-Verification**: Introduce a lightweight "Token Guard" for rapid secondary verification of audit conclusions. Add specialized spot-checks for "high-load reasoning degradation" patterns within the G4 Meta-Audit mechanism.

- **Tier 3: Dynamic Degradation and Rollback**: When monitoring metrics remain persistently abnormal, automatically degrade the review level for that task (e.g., from G3 to G1). Roll back the disputed context and transfer it to user adjudication, while recording the event for subsequent model optimization.

**Pending Validation**: The capability ceiling of the Token Guard is constrained by its own training data quality; the design of structural monitoring metrics is an open research question; the trigger thresholds for dynamic degradation require calibration across different domains.

### 4.6 Semantic Scene Filtering

**Design Motivation**: The Thought Layer should not indiscriminately perform logic review on all content. For emotional expressions, literary rhetoric, subjective value judgments, etc., mechanically executing logic review may not only produce unnecessary false positives but also impose inappropriate constraints on reasonable Execution Layer output.

**Scene Classification and Processing Strategy**:

| Information Type | Processing Strategy |
| - | - |
| **Emotional Expression** | Only perform intent alignment checks; do not initiate causal/fallacy scanning |
| **Literary/Rhetorical** | Only mark as "involving literary/rhetorical expression"; do not evaluate logical consistency |
| **Subjective Value Judgment** | Only check for significant contradictions with known user preferences |
| **Factual/Logical** | Initiate full review according to G0-G4 levels |


**Technical Implementation**: Utilize a lightweight intent recognition and text type classifier for pre-filtering before information enters the Thought Layer. The classifier itself can be internalized through fine-tuning the Thought Layer's own judgment capabilities.

**Pending Validation**: The classifier may misclassify ambiguous expressions (e.g., sarcasm, innuendo) as emotional or literary content, thereby incorrectly releasing content that should have been reviewed.

## 8. Conclusion and Outlook

The V3.1 draft, building upon the existing V3.0 architecture, proposes exploratory engineering deepening solutions targeting risks such as the alienation of Parameterized Cognitive Constraints, Thought Layer routing bypass, auditor self-degradation, and information weighting confusion. The draft's core principle—that any internal preference must be premised on being "open to questioning"—serves as the fundamental defense line preventing the architecture from sliding from "logic assurance" into a "new source of bias."

Frankly, the engineering feasibility and large-scale deployment effectiveness of several modules in this draft (audit preference vectors, structural monitoring metrics, dynamic coordination of dual-track weighting, etc.) still require prototype validation. The value of V3.1 lies in identifying the specific engineering challenges that need to be further addressed for V3.0's "endogenous immunity" direction, rather than providing a fully complete solution.

For task scenarios with high requirements for causal inference and logical rigor, the overall TLAA architecture still provides a scalable and evolvable design reference. Honesty means faithfulness to evidence, not submission to authority—the steadfast adherence to this principle is also the fundamental guarantee against the architecture's own alienation.
